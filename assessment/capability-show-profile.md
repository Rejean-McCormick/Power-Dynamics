# Capability Show Profile

The Capability Show Profile is the preferred format for demonstrating that a system is powerful **without collapsing capability into authority**.

For each capability claim, document:

## 1. Baseline limitation

What effective possibility is currently constrained? Examples: knowledge is fragmented, expertise is hard to discover, decisions are not executable, institutional memory is weak, exit is costly.

## 2. Resource and mechanism

What resources, components, protocols, roles, or institutions are introduced?

## 3. Capacity created

What organized ability becomes available?

## 4. Puissance demonstrated

Under realistic conditions, what can the actor or collective now effectively do that it could not do before, or could only do at much higher cost?

## 5. Composition gain

Which other capabilities become more useful because this capability exists?

## 6. Returns and compounding

What reusable assets are left behind? Does the action lower future cost, increase future quality, preserve memory, expand network access, or create shared infrastructure?

## 7. Capability-to-power transition

If the capability becomes widely relied upon, which actors' access, standing, visibility, allocation, interpretation, timing, dependency, or ability to act can be affected?

## 8. Conversion pathways

What legitimate conversions are expected? What cross-domain conversions should be blocked?

## 9. Counterpowers

Can affected actors verify, contest, substitute, exit, revoke, fork, or operate through an alternative branch?

## 10. Evidence and maturity

Separate:

- source-derived architecture;
- implemented technical evidence;
- demonstrated deployment evidence;
- analytical reconstruction;
- open questions.

The goal is to make the **Power Show falsifiable and inspectable**, not merely impressive.


## Power routing

A strong capability claim should now also state:

- who gains the capability;
- what new relational leverage may arise;
- whether the mechanism redirects any leverage away from a central operator;
- who receives the returns;
- what can be duplicated, shared, self-hosted, forked, or independently maintained;
- which rights remain centralized and why.

This prevents the Power Show from confusing **a powerful ecosystem** with **a powerful owner of the ecosystem**.
