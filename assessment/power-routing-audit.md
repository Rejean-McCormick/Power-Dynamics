# Power Routing Audit

Use this audit after identifying a system's generated puissance.

## 1. Capability destination

- Who becomes more capable?
- Is the capability reusable outside the original operator?
- Can participants teach, reproduce, or transfer it?

## 2. Authority destination

- Who gains approval, ranking, validation, certification, moderation, routing, update, or veto rights?
- Are those rights scoped?
- Can they be revoked or substituted?

## 3. Return destination

- Who receives money, data, reputation, network effects, knowledge, code, memory, and institutional access generated by collective activity?
- Do collective returns become shared assets or private concentration?

## 4. Optionality destination

- Who can leave?
- Who can self-host?
- Who can fork?
- Who can preserve identity, history, reputation, and knowledge after exit?

## 5. Interpretive destination

- Who defines categories, credibility policies, semantic mappings, reader policies, and defaults?
- Can alternative interpretations coexist?

## 6. Self-binding

- Are ethical or constitutional commitments public?
- Can affected actors cite them against the operator or founder?
- Are they connected to procedures or only aspirational prose?

## 7. Protocolization

- Which functions remain dependent on tacit founder knowledge?
- Which have explicit specifications?
- Can another maintainer or institution reproduce the function without unique permission?

## 8. Ownership decoupling

- Is a useful function inseparable from one brand, registry, identity provider, key, or operator?
- Could a compatible alternative become socially and technically viable?
