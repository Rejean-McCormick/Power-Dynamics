# Power Analysis Method

Use this sequence for any actor, institution, subsystem, or branch.

1. **Problem / baseline** — what incapacity, dependency, or coordination problem exists?
2. **Resources** — what stocks and assets are available?
3. **Potential** — what possibilities do they create?
4. **Capacity** — what is organized enough to be used?
5. **Puissance** — what can actually be mobilized now?
6. **Mobilization** — reservoir, release, throughput, channels, mediation, amplification.
7. **Recognition** — who makes capacity, claims, or contributions legible and actionable?
8. **Coordination** — how are needs, people, knowledge, timing, permissions, and resources matched?
9. **Power relations** — whose possibilities change, and how?
10. **Returns** — what assets are created and who receives them?
11. **Conversion pathways** — what can become what?
12. **Stacking / concentration** — what accumulates in one node?
13. **Dependency** — what becomes hard to substitute or exit?
14. **Counterpowers** — verification, contestation, substitution, exit, fork, branch mobility.
15. **Temporal analysis** — compounding, decay, succession, lock-in.
16. **Recognition / coordination wedges** — what capability is under-recognized or left inactive because of friction?
17. **Constitutional requirements** — what should be scoped, separated, portable, or reversible?
