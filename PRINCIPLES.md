# Principles

Power Dynamics begins from an affirmative premise: **people and collectives should be able to become dramatically more capable**. The constitutional problem is not how to suppress capability, but how to prevent successful capability from hardening into arbitrary, cross-domain, or terminal sovereignty.

These principles are therefore ordered from **generation** to **governance** to **evolution**.

## Generate puissance

1. **Increase effective possibility.** A system is empowering when it expands what actors can effectively understand, choose, build, coordinate, refuse, and accomplish.
2. **Turn dispersed resources into usable capability.** Knowledge, time, tools, money, relationships, infrastructure, and memory matter when actors can mobilize them.
3. **Collective puissance can be generated.** Coordinated capability can exceed the sum of isolated individual capabilities.
4. **Infrastructure should lower the cost of future action.** Good infrastructure crystallizes past effort into reusable capacity.
5. **Knowledge should become cumulative.** Action should leave reusable evidence, reasons, experience, and memory.
6. **Composition does not require ownership.** Functions can interoperate without one function becoming sovereign over the others.
7. **Capability should scale without requiring sovereignty to scale at the same rate.**

## Route and share power deliberately

8. **Power should be routed, not merely constrained after concentration.** Architecture can redirect leverage before it becomes terminal.
9. **Generated capability should preferentially become reusable capability for participants and communities where feasible.**
10. **Public ethical commitments can be a self-binding mechanism.** They do not prove compliance, but they give others stable grounds for criticism and contestation.
11. **Narrative power should carry its own limits.** Fiction and symbolic mediation can publish anti-domination, attribution, humility, and revision principles inside the transmission layer itself.
12. **Protocolization is power sharing.** Tacit founder mastery should progressively become explicit, testable, documented, and independently reproducible.
13. **Useful functions should be decoupled from exclusive ownership where possible.** Koali is a key test: credibility infrastructure should be duplicable, shareable, branchable, and capable of plural distributions rather than terminal personal ownership.
14. **Sharing capability does not require flattening expertise or responsibility.** The target is non-terminal asymmetry, not sameness.
15. **Returns are part of power routing.** Who receives knowledge, money, reputation, network, code, memory, and infrastructure generated by collective activity is a constitutional question.
16. **Exit, substitution, self-hosting, and forkability are power-sharing mechanisms because they transfer optionality to users.**
17. **The strongest center is one that can create capability beyond itself.**

## Maintain ontological discipline

18. **Resource ≠ Potential ≠ Capacity ≠ Puissance ≠ Power.**
19. **A resource is not automatically power.**
20. **Puissance is effective mobilizable capacity.**
21. **Power is relational and domain-specific.**
22. **Power to / with / through are primarily capability questions; power over is primarily a relational question.**
23. **Stock, reservoir, flow, reach, influence, authority, and sovereignty are not synonyms.**

## Preserve composition without silent conversion

24. **Different forms of puissance should remain composable without automatically converting into unrelated authority.**
25. **Expertise may inform; it does not automatically govern.**
26. **Money may fund; it does not automatically buy political equality.**
27. **Brand may attract attention; it does not become truth.**
28. **Technical administration may maintain infrastructure; it does not silently inherit political authority.**
29. **Credibility may improve discoverability; it does not become unquestionable legitimacy.**
30. **Execution may carry out a mandate; it does not silently redefine the mandate.**

## Make emerging power legible

31. **Architecture is governance before governance is written.**
32. **Every technical boundary is also a potential political boundary.**
33. **Every mechanism that distributes capability creates a new locus of power.**
34. **Stacking matters.** Several legitimate functions in one node can create cross-domain leverage.
35. **Capture is a conversion problem.** `FunctionalControl → CrossDomainPower` is a key warning pattern.
36. **Dependency is a gradient.** Formal choice is not effective choice when switching costs are prohibitive.
37. **Terminal power is a primary constitutional risk.**

## Preserve counterpower

38. **Verification + Contestation + Substitution + Exit** are foundational counterpowers.
39. **Formal exit is insufficient; exit must be effective.**
40. **Formal forkability is insufficient; branch mobility matters.**
41. **Revocability and reversibility are distinct.**
42. **Instance capture must not imply system capture.**
43. **Strong hubs are compatible with distributed capability when the whole remains independently reproducible.**

## Preserve epistemic humility

44. **No perfect human metric.**
45. **A reading is not the thing measured.** `Score ≠ person ≠ competence ≠ ethics ≠ truth`.
46. **Support is not evidence.**
47. **Dominance is not truth, quality, or final legitimacy.**
48. **Uncertainty, disagreement, and competing representations must remain representable.**
49. **Plurality is useful when it adds evidence, context, method, function, challenge, or synthesis.**

## Make capability regenerative

50. **Action leaves assets behind.**
51. **Who receives the returns matters.**
52. **Returns can compound future puissance.**
53. **Compounding can also compound concentration.**
54. **Shared infrastructure should convert collective activity into broader future capability where feasible.**
55. **Memory is a production factor, not merely an archive.**

## Keep the system evolvable

56. **Imperfection is permanent; authority need not be.**
57. **Variation is normal, not only an emergency response to capture.**
58. **Complementarity can be superior to convergence.**
59. **The stable kernel should protect evolution, not freeze outcomes.**
60. **A better branch should be able to demonstrate value, gather support, remain interoperable, and replace a dominant branch.**

## kOA orientation

61. **Strong puissance. Weak sovereignty.**
62. **StrongHub + DistributedCapability + ReplicableWhole.**
63. **AI assists under governance; AI does not silently inherit institutional sovereignty.**
64. **Semantic sovereignty requires practical control, not merely formal openness.**
65. **Founder independence is a resilience criterion, not a requirement to erase the founder.**
66. **Brand stewardship and protocol governance are distinct.**
67. **The system should remain capable of learning from its own alternatives.**
