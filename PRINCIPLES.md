# Principles

Power Dynamics begins from an affirmative premise: **people and collectives should be able to become dramatically more capable**. The constitutional problem is not how to suppress capability, but how to prevent successful capability from hardening into arbitrary, cross-domain, or terminal sovereignty.

These principles are therefore ordered from **generation** to **governance** to **evolution**.

## Generate puissance

1. **Increase effective possibility.** A system is empowering when it expands what actors can effectively understand, choose, build, coordinate, refuse, and accomplish.
2. **Turn dispersed resources into usable capability.** Knowledge, time, tools, money, relationships, infrastructure, and memory matter when actors can mobilize them.
3. **Collective puissance can be generated.** Coordinated capability can exceed the sum of isolated individual capabilities.
4. **Infrastructure should lower the cost of future action.** Good infrastructure crystallizes past effort into reusable capacity.
5. **Knowledge should become cumulative.** Action should leave reusable evidence, reasons, experience, and memory.
6. **Composition does not require ownership.** Functions can interoperate without one function becoming sovereign over the others.
7. **Capability should scale without requiring sovereignty to scale at the same rate.**

## Maintain ontological discipline

8. **Resource ≠ Potential ≠ Capacity ≠ Puissance ≠ Power.**
9. **A resource is not automatically power.**
10. **Puissance is effective mobilizable capacity.**
11. **Power is relational and domain-specific.**
12. **Power to / with / through are primarily capability questions; power over is primarily a relational question.**
13. **Stock, reservoir, flow, reach, influence, authority, and sovereignty are not synonyms.**

## Preserve composition without silent conversion

14. **Different forms of puissance should remain composable without automatically converting into unrelated authority.**
15. **Expertise may inform; it does not automatically govern.**
16. **Money may fund; it does not automatically buy political equality.**
17. **Brand may attract attention; it does not become truth.**
18. **Technical administration may maintain infrastructure; it does not silently inherit political authority.**
19. **Credibility may improve discoverability; it does not become unquestionable legitimacy.**
20. **Execution may carry out a mandate; it does not silently redefine the mandate.**

## Make emerging power legible

21. **Architecture is governance before governance is written.**
22. **Every technical boundary is also a potential political boundary.**
23. **Every mechanism that distributes capability creates a new locus of power.**
24. **Stacking matters.** Several legitimate functions in one node can create cross-domain leverage.
25. **Capture is a conversion problem.** `FunctionalControl → CrossDomainPower` is a key warning pattern.
26. **Dependency is a gradient.** Formal choice is not effective choice when switching costs are prohibitive.
27. **Terminal power is a primary constitutional risk.**

## Preserve counterpower

28. **Verification + Contestation + Substitution + Exit** are foundational counterpowers.
29. **Formal exit is insufficient; exit must be effective.**
30. **Formal forkability is insufficient; branch mobility matters.**
31. **Revocability and reversibility are distinct.**
32. **Instance capture must not imply system capture.**
33. **Strong hubs are compatible with distributed capability when the whole remains independently reproducible.**

## Preserve epistemic humility

34. **No perfect human metric.**
35. **A reading is not the thing measured.** `Score ≠ person ≠ competence ≠ ethics ≠ truth`.
36. **Support is not evidence.**
37. **Dominance is not truth, quality, or final legitimacy.**
38. **Uncertainty, disagreement, and competing representations must remain representable.**
39. **Plurality is useful when it adds evidence, context, method, function, challenge, or synthesis.**

## Make capability regenerative

40. **Action leaves assets behind.**
41. **Who receives the returns matters.**
42. **Returns can compound future puissance.**
43. **Compounding can also compound concentration.**
44. **Shared infrastructure should convert collective activity into broader future capability where feasible.**
45. **Memory is a production factor, not merely an archive.**

## Keep the system evolvable

46. **Imperfection is permanent; authority need not be.**
47. **Variation is normal, not only an emergency response to capture.**
48. **Complementarity can be superior to convergence.**
49. **The stable kernel should protect evolution, not freeze outcomes.**
50. **A better branch should be able to demonstrate value, gather support, remain interoperable, and replace a dominant branch.**

## kOA orientation

51. **Strong puissance. Weak sovereignty.**
52. **StrongHub + DistributedCapability + ReplicableWhole.**
53. **AI assists under governance; AI does not silently inherit institutional sovereignty.**
54. **Semantic sovereignty requires practical control, not merely formal openness.**
55. **Founder independence is a resilience criterion, not a requirement to erase the founder.**
56. **Brand stewardship and protocol governance are distinct.**
57. **The system should remain capable of learning from its own alternatives.**
