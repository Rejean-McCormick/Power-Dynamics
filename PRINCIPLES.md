# Principles

Power Dynamics begins from an affirmative premise: **people and collectives should be able to become dramatically more capable**. The constitutional problem is how to preserve that capability without allowing successful functions to harden into arbitrary, cross-domain, or terminal sovereignty.

## Generate immense but usable puissance

1. **Increase effective possibility.** expand what actors can realistically understand, choose, build, coordinate, refuse, preserve, and accomplish.
2. **Turn dispersed resources into mobilizable capability rather than merely accumulating resources.** 
3. **Collective puissance can be generated.** coordinated capability can exceed the sum of isolated capabilities.
4. **Infrastructure should crystallize past effort into reusable future capacity.** 
5. **Knowledge, memory, tools, and institutional learning should become cumulative.** 
6. **Capability should scale from individual to federation without requiring sovereignty to scale at the same rate.** 
7. **Composition does not require ownership.** interoperating functions need not collapse into one owner or authority.

## Keep domains explicit

8. **Political power, economic power, and monetary power are distinct even when they convert into one another.** 
9. **Political power begins before formal decision.** meaning, visibility, credibility, and agenda are political surfaces.
10. **Economic puissance concerns mobilization of scarce resources.** economic power concerns relational effects of that control.
11. **Monetary power concerns the infrastructure and conditions of exchange.** issuance, rails, liquidity, settlement, reserves, credit, and access.
12. **Administrative power can alter outcomes without changing formal policy.** 
13. **Semantic and epistemic power can shape the field in which later political decisions become intelligible.** 
14. **Every domain analysis should name resources, capability, relation, conversion paths, duration, and counterpowers.** 

## Maintain ontological discipline

15. **Resource ≠ Potential ≠ Capacity ≠ Puissance ≠ Power.** 
16. **A resource is not automatically power.** 
17. **Puissance is effective mobilizable capacity under real conditions.** 
18. **Power is relational and domain-specific.** 
19. **Power to / with / through are primarily capability questions.** power over is primarily a relational question.
20. **Stock, reservoir, flow, reach, influence, authority, legitimacy, and sovereignty are not synonyms.** 

## Expose conversion and stacking

21. **Different forms of puissance should remain composable without automatically converting into unrelated authority.** 
22. **Expertise may inform.** it does not automatically govern.
23. **Money may fund.** it does not automatically buy political standing.
24. **Brand may attract attention.** it does not become truth.
25. **Technical administration may maintain infrastructure.** it does not silently inherit political authority.
26. **Credibility may improve discoverability.** it does not become unquestionable legitimacy.
27. **Execution may carry out a mandate.** it does not silently redefine the mandate.
28. **Infrastructure may create capability.** it does not thereby own its users.
29. **Power stacking must be assessed across domains, not function by function.** 
30. **Capture is a conversion problem.** Functional Control → Cross-Domain Power.

## Route and share power deliberately

31. **Power should be routed, not merely constrained after concentration.** 
32. **Generated capability should preferentially become reusable capability for participants and communities where feasible.** 
33. **Public ethical commitments can be a self-binding mechanism.** they do not prove compliance but create stable grounds for criticism.
34. **Narrative power should carry its own limits through explicit anti-domination, attribution, humility, and revision principles.** 
35. **Protocolization is power sharing.** tacit founder mastery should become explicit, testable, documented, and independently reproducible.
36. **Useful functions should be decoupled from exclusive ownership where feasible.** 
37. **Koali is a key ownership-decoupling test.** credibility capability should be duplicable, shareable, branchable, and capable of plural distributions.
38. **Returns are part of power routing.** who receives knowledge, money, reputation, network, code, memory, and infrastructure is constitutional.
39. **Exit, substitution, self-hosting, and forkability distribute optionality.** 
40. **Sharing capability does not require flatness.** the target is non-terminal asymmetry.
41. **The strongest center is one that can create capability beyond itself.** 

## Make emerging power legible

42. **Architecture is governance before governance is written.** 
43. **Every technical boundary is also a potential political boundary.** 
44. **Every mechanism that distributes capability creates a new locus of power.** 
45. **Dependency is a gradient.** formal choice is not effective choice when switching costs are prohibitive.
46. **Terminal power is a primary constitutional risk.** 
47. **Functional centrality is not automatically domination, but centrality plus dependency plus weak counterpower can become domination.** 
48. **Instance capture must not imply system capture.** 

## Preserve counterpower

49. **Verification + Contestation + Substitution + Exit are foundational counterpowers.** 
50. **Formal exit is insufficient.** exit must be effective.
51. **Formal forkability is insufficient.** branch mobility matters.
52. **Revocability and reversibility are distinct.** 
53. **Strong hubs are compatible with distributed capability when the whole remains independently reproducible.** 
54. **Portability must include the artifacts, history, provenance, evidence, and configuration needed for meaningful continuity.** 

## Preserve epistemic humility

55. **No measure of competence, ethics, credibility, contribution, or legitimacy should be treated as perfectly complete or permanently final.** 
56. **Score ≠ person ≠ competence ≠ ethics ≠ truth.** 
57. **Support is not evidence.** 
58. **Dominance is not truth, quality, or final legitimacy.** 
59. **Uncertainty, disagreement, and competing representations must remain representable.** 
60. **Plurality is useful when it adds evidence, context, method, function, challenge, translation, or synthesis.** 

## Make capability regenerative

61. **Action should leave assets behind.** 
62. **Who receives the returns matters.** 
63. **Returns can compound future puissance.** 
64. **Compounding can also compound concentration and must be audited.** 
65. **Shared infrastructure should convert collective activity into broader future capability where feasible.** 
66. **Memory is a production factor, not merely an archive.** 

## Keep the system evolvable

67. **Imperfection is permanent.** authority need not be.
68. **Variation is normal institutional experimentation, not only an emergency response to capture.** 
69. **Complementarity can be superior to convergence.** 
70. **The stable kernel should protect evolution, not freeze outcomes.** 
71. **A better branch should be able to demonstrate value, gather support, remain interoperable, and influence or replace a dominant branch.** 
72. **Improvement claims should state the dimension, beneficiaries, conditions, evidence, and trade-offs.** 

## kOA constitutional orientation

73. **Strong distributed puissance.** Weak sovereignty.
74. **StrongHub + DistributedCapability + ReplicableWhole.** 
75. **AI assists under governance.** AI does not silently inherit institutional sovereignty.
76. **Semantic sovereignty requires practical control, not merely formal openness.** 
77. **Founder independence is a resilience criterion, not a requirement to erase the founder.** 
78. **Brand stewardship and protocol governance are distinct.** 
79. **The system should remain capable of learning from its own alternatives.** 



## Adversarial resilience

80. **Founder fallibility is expected.** A durable system must survive both founder absence and founder error.
81. **Counterpower should be rehearsable.** Formal opposition rights are weaker than practiced, socially legitimate opposition pathways.
82. **Forkability does not imply epistemic equivalence.** Incompatible worldviews may coexist while evidence, provenance, contradiction, uncertainty, and lineage remain comparable.
83. **Apparent multiplicity is not independent confirmation.** Source and credibility systems should distinguish surface count from causal independence where provenance permits.
84. **A reference branch must remain a reference, not a monopoly on reality.** Stewardship can maintain coherence without eliminating the right to branch, contest, reinterpret, or simulate alternatives.
