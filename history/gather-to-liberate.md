# Gather to Liberate in the long history of power

The recurring historical problem is not simply concentrated wealth or office. It is **dependence on institutions that control access to knowledge, coordination, resources, adjudication, or legitimate recognition**.

A proposed cycle to investigate is:

```text
productive capabilities grow
  -> surplus, assets and institutional power may concentrate
  -> control of rules, attention, credit and legitimacy may compound
  -> hardship, elite conflict, state weakness or legitimacy crisis may arise
  -> reforms, institutional replacement, revolution or authoritarian closure
  -> new centers of authority form
```

This is a research heuristic, **not** an inevitable law or proof of current revolutionary conditions. Material welfare can improve while expectations and visibility of inequality rise. More information does not automatically yield better discernment; narrative selection, coordination, and institutions still matter.

## The civilizer analogy and its limit

Some traditions describe civilizing figures who transmit fire, agriculture, calendars, skills or teachings. Quetzalcóatl belongs to diverse Mesoamerican religious and historical traditions and cannot be reduced to a generic symbol of technological progress. King Klown is a contemporary project persona; comparison is **symbolic**, not historical descent, equivalence, supernatural claim, or moral ranking.

The productive analogy is the **transfer of capability**. The dangerous pattern is for followers or institutions to turn a teaching into unchallengeable authority. The King / Klown tension can be read as continuous institution-building paired with ongoing permission to question the institution itself.

## Beyond divide-and-conquer

`Divide and Conquer` captures control through separation and coordination asymmetry. `Gather to Liberate` instead proposes reducing dependence by connecting learning, trust assessment, joint decision, practical execution and institutional memory.

The critical difference from simply forming a large unified movement is **plurality with exit**: communities may disagree, use different recognition standards, maintain different instances, and still exchange knowledge through open interfaces.

## Polycentric institutional evolution

In a successful forkable ecology, a discredited institution need not be violently seized to become less influential. Alternative bodies could run independent instances, establish their own policies and earn voluntary recognition while interacting through common formats. Traditional legal authority, public mandates, reputation and unlicensed assets cannot be cloned by copying code. Where legal monopoly is involved, law and politics remain indispensable.

**Historical thesis to test:** institutional contestability can be an additional pathway of social evolution alongside revolution and reform, if exit is genuinely affordable and external recognition is not captured by network effects.

## Empirical challenge

Evidence for this thesis would include independent community deployments; demonstrable migration after institutional failure; continued interoperability between disagreeing branches; robust recognition of minority evidence; transparent revocation and appeals; resistance to standards monopolies; and observable participant autonomy without founder mediation.

Without those outcomes, the proposal remains a compelling normative aspiration, not an established historical innovation.
