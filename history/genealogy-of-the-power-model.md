# Genealogy of the Power Model

The corpus can be read as an evolution in the theory of power.

1. **Dependency diagnosis** — modern domination operates through debt, time, procedure, complexity, desire and institutions that narrow effective freedom.
2. **Agency and transmission** — language, narrative, association and creative action can expand what people can imagine and coordinate.
3. **Strong corrective institutions** — some earlier economic proposals answer inequality with large centralized mechanisms.
4. **Knowledge-to-action architecture** — kOA formalizes collective capability through structured transitions and memory.
5. **Non-domination and semantic sovereignty** — more recent texts make autonomy, local control, contestation and bounded authority explicit.
6. **Evolutionary plurality** — the emerging reconstruction recognizes that no measurement or branch is final and that improvement should remain distributed.

The repo preserves this genealogy instead of pretending every document expresses one timeless doctrine.
