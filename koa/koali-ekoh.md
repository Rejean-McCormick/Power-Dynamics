# Koali / EkoH

Koali and EkoH address a problem of **latent competence and coordination friction**: people may possess useful experience, expertise, willingness, or local knowledge that is difficult for others to discover, verify, or mobilize.

The intended transformation is:

```text
capacity / contribution
→ evidence
→ legibility
→ contextual credibility
→ discoverability
→ opportunity
→ coordination
→ participation
→ result
→ new evidence
```

## Koali as a coordination layer

The design direction is to route calls according to bounded context rather than require constant self-promotion or one centralized human coordinator. Relevant variables can include:

```text
need
+ capability / evidence
+ availability
+ willingness
+ task fit
+ confidentiality preferences
+ reliability where relevant
+ local / institutional constraints
```

The output should be a set of viable pathways or invitations, not automatic sovereignty over the participants.

## EkoH as contextual recognition

The constitutional limit is explicit:

> **Score ≠ person ≠ competence ≠ ethics ≠ truth ≠ legitimacy ≠ sovereignty.**

EkoH should therefore prefer **profiles of evidence and contextual readings** over one universal scalar score.

An ethics or safety dimension may be relevant for a specific role, but it should not become a general moral ranking that silently controls unrelated access. Finance, access to children or vulnerable people, technical operations, public authority, and artistic collaboration require different evidence, rules, and legitimate decision authorities.

Signals should be:

- domain-bounded;
- evidence-linked;
- versioned;
- contestable / correctable;
- privacy-aware;
- minimally disclosed for the task;
- portable where feasible;
- time-sensitive where appropriate.

## Credit / credibility analogy

EkoH is not money and should not be treated as financial credit. But there is a useful functional analogy:

```text
credibility: evidence → trust → opportunity / access
credit: expected repayment + safeguards → present resources
```

Both can reduce uncertainty and convert recognized confidence into present capacity. The distinction matters because financial credit also depends on repayment capacity, collateral, contracts, liquidity, policy, and market conditions.

## Power-sharing direction

A credibility layer becomes dangerous if useful recognition is inseparable from one owner, one operator, one permanent scoring policy, or one identity rail.

The current design direction is therefore to make Koali **duplicable, shareable, branchable, and capable of plural distributions**, with evidence portability and explicit policy identity rather than terminal proprietary recognition authority.
