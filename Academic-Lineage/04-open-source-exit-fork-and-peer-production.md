# Exit, Voice, Forking, and Peer Production

## Albert O. Hirschman — exit and voice

Hirschman's classic distinction asks how members respond when an organization deteriorates: they may **exit** or use **voice** to attempt repair.

**Correspondence:** Power Dynamics deliberately combines contestation and credible exit rather than treating either as sufficient alone.

**Design implication:**

- voice without exit can leave participants captive;
- exit without voice can cause unnecessary fragmentation;
- formal exit without portable assets may be practically meaningless.

Reference: Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty*, Harvard University Press, 1970.

## Nyman & Lindman — forking as governance

Nyman and Lindman explicitly analyze code forking as a governance and sustainability mechanism across software, community, and ecosystem levels.

**Correspondence:** forkability, branch mobility, incumbent replacement, and the idea that the **possibility** of a fork can constrain maintainers before a fork occurs.

Reference: Linus Nyman and Juho Lindman, “Code Forking, Governance, and Sustainability in Open Source Software,” *Technology Innovation Management Review*, January 2013. https://www.timreview.ca/article/644

## O'Mahony & Ferraro — authority can emerge without conventional ownership

Their study of an open-source community shows the development of formal authority combined with democratic mechanisms and evolving conceptions of legitimate leadership.

**Correspondence:** a commons still needs governance; `open` does not mean `authority-free`. The relevant question is whether authority remains bounded, contestable, and revisable.

Reference: Siobhán O'Mahony and Fabrizio Ferraro, “The Emergence of Governance in an Open Source Community,” *Academy of Management Journal* 50(5), 2007. https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.27169153

## Yochai Benkler — commons-based peer production

Benkler's analysis of Linux and peer production demonstrates a mode of large-scale production that is neither simply firm hierarchy nor price-mediated market organization.

**Correspondence:** independent contributors can take responsibility for branches, modules, documentation, institutions, or deployments without becoming employees of a central owner.

Reference: Yochai Benkler, “Coase's Penguin, or, Linux and The Nature of the Firm,” *Yale Law Journal* 112, 2002. https://yalelawjournal.org/article/coases-penguin-or-linux-and-the-nature-of-the-firm

## Founder decentering as protocolization

Power Dynamics goes beyond “the founder promises not to interfere.” It seeks conversion of founder-specific capability into:

- explicit schemas;
- reproducible builds;
- documentation;
- public interfaces;
- branch rights;
- independent maintenance capacity;
- portable data and artifacts.

The academic lineage above supports the importance of these mechanisms while also warning that informal reputation, scarce expertise, release keys, brand ownership, and installed-base dominance can preserve founder power after formal control has been removed.
