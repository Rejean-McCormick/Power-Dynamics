# Capabilities, Concerted Power, and the Risks of Legibility

Three academic traditions sharpen the normative stakes of Power Dynamics: **capabilities**, **collective agency**, and the dangers of making society legible through simplified administrative categories.

## Amartya Sen — capability and substantive freedom

Sen's capability approach shifts attention from resources alone toward what people are **actually able to do and be**. Resources matter partly through the substantive freedoms they make possible.

**Correspondence:** Power Dynamics' concept of **effective possibility** is structurally close to a capability-oriented question: possession is not enough; what matters is the real action space available under actual constraints.

**Design implication:** a system should not measure empowerment only through nominal access. Ask whether participants can actually use, contest, exit, reproduce, and benefit from the capability.

Reference: Amartya Sen, *Development as Freedom*, 1999/2000. https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/163962/development-as-freedom-by-amartya-sen/

## Hannah Arendt — power as acting in concert

Arendt distinguishes power from simple coercion or violence and ties political power to a group's capacity to **act in concert**.

**Correspondence:** this is a useful academic neighbor to **generated collective puissance**: composition can create a capacity at group scale that no isolated participant possesses.

**Limit:** Arendt's political concept of power is normatively and philosophically distinct from the broader Power Dynamics ontology.

Reference: Hannah Arendt, *On Violence*, 1970.

## James C. Scott — legibility can become domination

Scott's *Seeing Like a State* studies failures of high-modernist schemes that simplified complex local realities into administratively legible categories and then governed through those abstractions.

**Correspondence:** kOA deliberately increases legibility — schemas, classifications, provenance, metrics, workflows, registries, institutional grammar. That is a capability **and a danger**.

**Design implication:** semantic clarity must not erase local knowledge or turn the reference model into compulsory reality.

The anti-capture answer is not “avoid legibility,” but combine legibility with:

- local override / interpretation;
- plural schemas where necessary;
- provenance and uncertainty;
- reversible mappings;
- branch alternatives;
- explicit scope;
- user-visible disagreement.

Reference: James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to Improve the Human Condition Have Failed*, Yale University Press, 1998/1999. https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300078152/seeing-like-a-state/

## Synthesis

These three traditions create a useful triangle:

```text
Sen:     What can people actually do?
Arendt:  What can people do together?
Scott:   What happens when institutions simplify people in order to coordinate them?
```

Power Dynamics can use that triangle as a normative test:

> **Increase effective individual and collective capability without making the legibility infrastructure a new sovereign.**
