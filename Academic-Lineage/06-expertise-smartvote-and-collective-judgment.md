# Expertise, SmartVote, and Collective Judgment

This is the most direct academic lineage for the SmartVote principle:

> **Expertise should be bounded by domain and used as an advisory reading rather than silently converted into universal political sovereignty.**

## Chi, Feltovich & Glaser — experts structure problems differently within a domain

Their classic physics study found systematic differences between expert and novice representations: experts tended to organize problems around deeper domain principles rather than surface features.

**Correspondence:** expert standing should be attached to a **domain / problem class**, not treated as a universal personal property.

Reference: Michelene T. H. Chi, Paul J. Feltovich, and Robert Glaser, “Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices,” *Cognitive Science* 5(2), 1981. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

## Ericsson & Lehmann — expert performance is adapted to domain-specific constraints

Their broad review emphasizes domain-specific cognitive, perceptual-motor, and physiological adaptations behind expert performance.

**Correspondence:** a universal “expert score” would collapse heterogeneous competence into an invalid abstraction. EkoH / SmartVote credibility should be scoped to demonstrable domains and tasks.

Reference: K. Anders Ericsson and Andreas C. Lehmann, “Expert and Exceptional Performance: Evidence of Maximal Adaptation to Task Constraints,” *Annual Review of Psychology* 47, 1996. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.47.1.273

## Alvin Goldman — the layperson's expert-selection problem

Goldman analyzes how nonexperts can rationally decide which experts to trust when they cannot directly evaluate the technical subject matter.

**Correspondence:** SmartVote should not merely expose an `expert` badge. It should make evidence of expertise, track record, relevant credentials / contribution, disagreement, and source relations legible.

Reference: Alvin I. Goldman, “Experts: Which Ones Should You Trust?”, *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 63(1), 2001. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2001.tb00093.x

## Roger Cooke — calibrated expert judgment

Cooke develops methods for eliciting, evaluating, and weighting expert judgments, including performance-based calibration rather than reliance on status alone.

**Correspondence:** `expertise → weighted advisory reading` can be grounded in observable performance and calibration rather than title-based authority.

Reference: Roger M. Cooke, *Experts in Uncertainty: Opinion and Subjective Probability in Science*, Oxford University Press, 1991. https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780195064650.001.0001

## Budescu & Chen — identify contributors who improve the crowd

Budescu and Chen show that aggregation can be improved by identifying judges whose forecasts consistently make positive contributions relative to the group.

**Correspondence:** reputation may be **performance-sensitive** and task-sensitive rather than merely social prestige.

Reference: David V. Budescu and Eva Chen, “Identifying Expertise to Extract the Wisdom of Crowds,” *Management Science* 61(2), 2015. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1909

## Mannes, Soll & Larrick — select crowds

Their work shows that in some estimation settings, averaging judgments from a carefully selected small group can be robust and accurate.

**Correspondence:** Power Dynamics should allow multiple aggregation lenses rather than assuming either `all votes always equal epistemically` or `single best expert decides`.

Reference: Albert E. Mannes, Jack B. Soll, and Richard P. Larrick, “The Wisdom of Select Crowds,” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 107(2), 2014. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036677

## Hong & Page — diversity can outperform selection of individually strongest solvers

Under their model's conditions, a diverse group of problem solvers can outperform a group selected only for individual ability.

**Correspondence:** SmartVote should not turn expertise weighting into an anti-diversity filter. Diversity of representations and heuristics can itself be an epistemic resource.

Reference: Lu Hong and Scott E. Page, “Groups of Diverse Problem Solvers Can Outperform Groups of High-Ability Problem Solvers,” *PNAS* 101(46), 2004. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0403723101

## Social choice: Arrow, Gibbard, and Satterthwaite

Social choice theory supplies an important warning: there is no universal voting mechanism that simultaneously satisfies every attractive property under unrestricted conditions, and broad classes of voting systems are vulnerable to strategic manipulation.

**Correspondence:** SmartVote should present itself as a **plural, inspectable set of readings and decision aids**, not as a mathematically perfect replacement for politics.

References:
- Kenneth J. Arrow, *Social Choice and Individual Values*, 1951/1963.
- Allan Gibbard, “Manipulation of Voting Schemes: A General Result,” *Econometrica* 41(4), 1973. https://doi.org/10.2307/1914083
- Mark A. Satterthwaite, “Strategy-proofness and Arrow's Conditions,” *Journal of Economic Theory* 10(2), 1975. https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(75)90050-2

## SmartVote design implications

The literature supports several strong constraints:

1. **Domain-bounded expertise** — no universal expert authority.
2. **Performance evidence** — title or prestige is not enough.
3. **Plural aggregation** — different lenses can answer different questions.
4. **Diversity preservation** — selecting only high-status experts can destroy useful heterogeneity.
5. **Political firewall** — epistemic weighting does not automatically purchase political sovereignty.
6. **Manipulation awareness** — every ranking / voting mechanism exposes gaming surfaces.
7. **Baseline visibility** — raw democratic readings should remain visible when advisory lenses are added.

None of these references validates a particular SmartVote weighting formula. They provide **design constraints and testable failure modes**.
