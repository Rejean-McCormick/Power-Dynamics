# Network Power, Brokerage, Diffusion, and Social Capital

## Linton Freeman — centrality is multidimensional

Freeman clarified distinct concepts of centrality rather than treating “central” as one thing.

**Correspondence:** network / affiliation power should be measured through several structural lenses rather than a single universal score.

Reference: Linton C. Freeman, “Centrality in Social Networks: Conceptual Clarification,” *Social Networks* 1(3), 1978–79. https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-8733(78)90021-7

## Phillip Bonacich — centrality is not automatically power

Bonacich explicitly addresses cases in which network centrality and power diverge.

**Correspondence:** Power Dynamics should resist `many connections = sovereignty`. Dependency structure and the positions of one's neighbors matter.

Reference: Phillip Bonacich, “Power and Centrality: A Family of Measures,” *American Journal of Sociology* 92(5), 1987. https://doi.org/10.1086/228631

## Mark Granovetter — weak ties and cross-cluster diffusion

Weak ties can connect otherwise separated social clusters and play an outsized role in information diffusion, mobility, and community organization.

**Correspondence:** mobilization capacity is not only a function of audience size or strong affiliation. **Bridging ties** can matter disproportionately for spreading initiatives across communities.

Reference: Mark S. Granovetter, “The Strength of Weak Ties,” *American Journal of Sociology* 78(6), 1973. https://doi.org/10.1086/225469

## Ronald Burt — structural holes and brokerage

Burt's structural-hole theory treats gaps between otherwise disconnected actors as opportunities for brokerage, information access, and strategic advantage.

**Correspondence:** identify actors or services whose brokerage position becomes a hidden allocation or gatekeeping surface.

Reference: Ronald S. Burt, *Structural Holes: The Social Structure of Competition*, Harvard University Press, 1992.

## James Coleman — social capital as a resource for action

Coleman describes obligations, expectations, information channels, and norms as forms of social capital that enable action.

**Correspondence:** Power Dynamics' broad resource → capacity model can explicitly recognize network relations and trust structures as **resources that alter effective possibility**.

Reference: James S. Coleman, “Social Capital in the Creation of Human Capital,” *American Journal of Sociology* 94, 1988. https://doi.org/10.1086/228943

## Audit consequence

A network power audit should inspect at least:

- centrality;
- dependency / substitutability;
- brokerage;
- bridge positions;
- control of discovery / introductions;
- access to high-trust clusters;
- concentration of communication channels;
- ability to route opportunities to others.

Network maps show topology. They do **not** by themselves show legitimacy, authority, or domination.
