# Canonical Glossary

This glossary is normative for repository terminology. It keeps the **kOA Power Show** expressive without allowing broad language such as “powerful” to collapse distinct analytical states. When a term is used differently in a source document, preserve the source meaning and explicitly distinguish it from the repository definition.

## Canonical distinctions

| Do not conflate | Canonical distinction |
|---|---|
| resource / power | a resource supports action; power is relational |
| potential / capacity | potential is unrealized possibility; capacity is organized ability |
| capacity / puissance | capacity can exist without being effectively mobilizable; puissance is effective under real conditions |
| puissance / power | puissance produces effects; power shapes another actor's effective possibilities |
| stock / flow | stock is accumulated; flow is movement or throughput |
| reservoir / power | a reservoir retains resources or options; it does not store relational power as a substance |
| score / competence | a score is a governed reading of evidence, not competence itself |
| credibility / truth | credibility affects trust; it does not establish truth by itself |
| support / evidence | support is social endorsement; evidence supports a claim or evaluation |
| dominance / quality | dominance describes position or adoption; quality is multidimensional and contestable |
| forkability / branch mobility | a fork may be possible while remaining unable to attract users, evidence, interoperability, or institutional support |
| centrality / sovereignty | a hub may be functionally central without possessing total or terminal authority |

### French / English convention

The repository deliberately keeps **puissance** in French even inside English prose when precision matters. English *power* collapses several meanings that this framework separates.

- **potential / potentiel** — unrealized possibility;
- **capacity / capacité** — organized ability;
- **puissance** — effectively mobilizable capacity;
- **power / pouvoir relationnel** — relational shaping of another actor's effective possibilities.

## Power Show terms

**kOA Power Show** — the presentation layer of Power Dynamics that maps the forms of effective capability kOA can generate, compose, preserve, distribute, and evolve before analyzing the power relations created by successful capability.

**Capability spectrum** — the set of distinct forms of potential, capacity, and puissance available to an actor or system across domains. In kOA this includes epistemic, semantic, cognitive, collective, network, credibility, decision, operational, mnemonic, technical, infrastructural, federative, exit, and evolutionary capability.

**Capability loop** — a sequence in which one capability feeds the next. The canonical kOA loop is `Know → Choose → Act → Remember → Know better`.

**Closed capability loop** — a capability loop in which outputs are sufficiently preserved and fed back as inputs that the system can learn from prior action rather than repeatedly restarting from near-zero institutional memory. “Closed” does not mean perfectly self-contained.

**Composition engine** — shorthand for the architecture through which distinct capabilities become interoperable inputs to one another, creating higher-order or generated puissance.

**Composition gain** — additional effective capability created because separate functions interoperate. It is the difference between possessing useful components and possessing a coherent capability system.

**Compounding engine** — shorthand for feedback loops in which action produces reusable returns that lower the cost, increase the quality, or expand the scope of future action.

**Capability infrastructure** — technical, semantic, institutional, or procedural infrastructure whose primary effect is to make future action easier, more reliable, more composable, or more reusable.

**Capability surface** — a function or interface through which a system increases what actors can effectively do. A capability surface can later become a power surface if control over it changes other actors’ effective possibilities.

**Power surface** — a function, interface, registry, role, default, gate, workflow, standard, or infrastructure boundary through which control can shape other actors’ access, standing, visibility, allocation, timing, interpretation, or ability to act.

**Capability-to-power transition** — the point at which successful puissance becomes relationally consequential for other actors. This transition is central to the repository: the same function can be empowering as capability and politically consequential as a controlled gate.

## Ontology

**Resource** — anything available to an actor or system that can support future action: money, time, knowledge, code, rights, relationships, reputation, data, infrastructure, identity, property, attention, memory, or other conditions.

**Asset** — a resource that has been accumulated, stabilized, owned, controlled, recognized, or made reusable enough to support future action.

**Stock** — an accumulated quantity or body of resources/assets at a point in time. A stock is descriptive; it is not automatically puissance or power.

**Potential** — unrealized effective possibility preserved or created by resources and conditions.

**Capacity** — an organized ability to perform a class of actions under specified conditions.

**Puissance** — capacity that is effectively mobilizable under real conditions to produce effects. Puissance is the preferred term for what English often calls *power to*, *power with*, or *power through* when no relational control over others is implied.

**Power** — a relational capacity, grounded in puissance, through which one actor, group, institution, or infrastructure shapes another's effective possibilities.

**Power relation** — a relation in which another actor's access, options, standing, visibility, allocation, authority, incentives, categories, timing, dependencies, or ability to act are materially changed.

**Effect** — a realized change produced by action, mediation, or a power relation.

**Return** — a reusable asset created or preserved by an action or effect and available for future cycles.

**Effective possibility** — what an actor can realistically understand, choose, refuse, obtain, build, coordinate, contest, preserve, or leave under actual material, institutional, temporal, informational, and social conditions.

## Modes of capability and relation

**Puissance d'agir / power to** — effective ability to accomplish, understand, build, decide, refuse, learn, create, or otherwise act.

**Collective puissance / power with** — effective capability generated through coordination among multiple actors.

**Infrastructure-mediated puissance / power through** — effective capability enabled, stored, routed, amplified, or made reusable through infrastructure.

**Power over** — an asymmetric family of power relations in which one actor can condition, constrain, block, allocate, define, authorize, or otherwise shape another's possibilities. It is not the definition of all power.

**Mutual power** — a reciprocal power relation in which actors alter one another's possibilities in ways that can expand joint capacity.

**Generated puissance** — new effective capacity created by composition that did not exist in any participant separately.

**Latent puissance** — capacity that could be mobilized but is currently inactive because of timing, coordination, information, access, or other constraints.

## Dynamics

**Formation** — the process through which resources become organized capacity and then effective puissance.

**Mobilization** — the activation of available resources or puissance into action, exposure, coordination, or effects.

**Reservoir** — a configuration in which accumulated resources or reusable assets are retained for future activation. A reservoir is not stored relational power.

**Retention** — preserving a resource, asset, option, or capability rather than immediately committing or releasing it.

**Release** — activation, disclosure, deployment, spending, publication, delegation, or other transition from retained resource to active use.

**Release puissance** — effective ability to control the timing, sequencing, form, and channels through which a retained resource is activated.

**Flow** — movement of mobilized resources, signals, actions, or effects through a system.

**Throughput** — the practical rate at which a system can process, deploy, distribute, or convert resources and puissance into effects.

**Mediation** — selection, framing, routing, translation, ranking, interpretation, packaging, contextualization, or transformation between source and affected actors.

**Amplification** — an increase in scale or intensity of an effect relative to the initiating input because of a network, platform, institution, technology, or channel.

**Bottleneck** — a constrained passage that limits throughput or access to a necessary transformation.

**Bottleneck power** — relational power created when an actor controls a passage that others must use to convert resources or puissance into effects.

**Composition** — combining distinct resources, capacities, or forms of puissance to create a higher-order capability.

**Conversion** — transformation of a resource, asset, capacity, puissance, or relational position into another form.

**Conversion pathway** — an identifiable sequence through which one form becomes another, e.g. `knowledge → credibility → visibility → opportunity`.

**Conversion firewall** — a rule or architecture that permits legitimate composition while blocking automatic conversion into unrelated authority.

**Reinvestment** — using returns to create or amplify future capacity and puissance.

**Compounding puissance** — growth in future effective capacity caused by reinvestment and reuse across cycles.

**Power yield** — the set of returns, effects, and relational positions generated by a given exercise of puissance or power. It should not be reduced to a single scalar metric.

**Concentration** — increasing share of relevant resources, puissance, or relational control accumulating in one actor, node, branch, or institution.

**Stacking** — accumulation of several distinct functions in the same actor or node, producing cross-domain leverage not visible in any function separately.

**Capture** — durable conversion of legitimate functional control into influence or control outside its intended mandate.

**Cross-domain conversion** — movement of advantage from one domain into another, e.g. economic assets becoming political influence or technical administration becoming agenda control.

**Diversification** — distribution of capability or governance across multiple actors, implementations, branches, domains, or institutions.

**Decay** — reduction in usefulness, credibility, adoption, capability, legitimacy, liquidity, network position, or other components of puissance over time.

**Dissipation** — loss of potential returns because knowledge, experience, attention, coordination, or other value is not preserved or reused.

**Transfer** — movement of resources, capacity, authority, relational position, or responsibility between actors or institutions.

**Succession** — governed transfer of roles, stewardship, keys, mandates, or interpretive responsibility across time.

## Dependence, domination, and counterpower

**Dependency** — reliance on an actor, system, resource, or infrastructure whose substitution or exit is materially costly, slow, unavailable, or illegible.

**Dependency gradient** — degree of dependency, determined by alternatives, switching cost, time, portability, recognition, rights, knowledge, network effects, and other factors.

**Domination** — exposure to arbitrary or insufficiently contestable relational power, especially where dependency is high and counterpowers are weak.

**Counterpower** — capacity that can verify, contest, substitute, exit, revoke, reverse, fork, or otherwise alter the terms of a power relation.

**Verification** — ability to independently inspect claims, artifacts, processes, identities, signatures, calculations, or compliance.

**Contestation** — ability to challenge a claim, score, decision, rule, ranking, authority, or implementation through a meaningful process.

**Substitution** — ability to replace a provider, branch, implementation, authority root, workflow, or institution with a viable alternative.

**Exit** — ability to leave a relationship or system while preserving enough assets, rights, data, identity, reputation, and continuity for exit to be meaningful.

**Formal exit** — nominal legal or technical ability to leave.

**Effective exit** — practical ability to leave at acceptable cost without disproportionate loss of essential capability.

**Forkability** — ability to create a divergent implementation, policy, ontology, or branch from an existing lineage.

**Effective forkability** — forkability plus the practical ability to operate, interoperate, attract support, preserve provenance, migrate assets, and become viable.

**Revocability** — ability to withdraw authority or permission.

**Reversibility** — ability to undo or repair the effects of an earlier action or authority. Revocability does not guarantee reversibility.

**Terminal power** — relational power whose capture, failure, or abuse cannot be effectively substituted, reversed, contested, or exited.

**Instance capture** — capture of one deployment, organization, branch, or institution.

**System capture** — capture of the broader architecture such that meaningful alternatives cannot continue, interoperate, or emerge.

## Branch ecology and evolutionary governance

**Branch** — a distinct version, implementation, policy, ontology, or interpretation with enough lineage and provenance to be compared with alternatives.

**Lineage** — traceable relationship among versions, forks, sources, transformations, and derived artifacts.

**Branch mobility** — practical ability of a branch to become discoverable, credible, supported, interoperable, adopted, and capable of influencing or replacing dominant branches.

**Dominance** — a branch's current advantage in adoption, support, visibility, compatibility, institutional recognition, network effects, or infrastructure. Dominance does not imply truth or optimality.

**Complementarity** — additional value produced when distinct branches, perspectives, or implementations cover different evidence, contexts, scales, or functions without requiring a universal winner.

**Redundant plurality** — multiple branches that add little new evidence, context, method, function, or interoperability value.

**Meaningful variation** — branch difference that creates identifiable added value or tests a consequential alternative.

**Evolutionary governance** — governance in which consequential rules, policies, interpretations, and implementations remain open to explicit variation, demonstration, comparison, adoption, recombination, amendment, and replacement.

**Stable kernel** — shared minimal contracts, formats, provenance rules, or interoperability requirements that permit divergence without fragmentation.

**Evolving branch** — a branch whose policies, readings, implementations, or governance may change while preserving lineage and interoperability where feasible.

**Recombination** — integration of useful elements from multiple branches into a new branch or later version.

**Replacement** — transition in which an alternative becomes more adopted, authoritative, useful, or institutionally supported than an earlier dominant branch.

## Epistemic, semantic, and credibility terms

**Epistemic puissance** — effective ability to produce, organize, validate, compare, preserve, or reuse knowledge.

**Semantic puissance** — effective ability to create, maintain, translate, contest, and evolve categories, ontologies, definitions, and relations of meaning.

**Semantic power** — relational power created when control over categories, definitions, ontologies, thresholds, or classifications shapes how others can understand, qualify, or act.

**Semantic sovereignty** — practical capacity of a person or community to govern the terms, categories, ontologies, and knowledge structures through which it generates and shares meaning without arbitrary dependence on external authorities.

**Credibility asset** — accumulated evidence or recognition that makes a person, branch, claim, or institution more likely to be trusted in a defined context.

**Credibility signal** — governed reading of evidence about expertise, contribution, conduct, reliability, or other criteria. It is not the person or competence itself.

**Domain-bounded expertise** — expertise whose relevance and influence are limited to explicit domains rather than generalized into universal authority.

**Discoverability puissance** — effective ability to make relevant content, people, or branches findable.

**Discoverability power** — relational power created when control of discovery materially shapes what others see, reuse, fund, cite, learn, adopt, or build.

**Pre-political power** — power exercised through meaning, knowledge, visibility, credibility, agenda, and framing before a formal decision is made.

**Mnemonic puissance** — effective ability to preserve and reuse knowledge, reasons, decisions, actions, failures, and outcomes across time.

**Mnemonic power** — relational power created when control of collective memory shapes future interpretation, precedent, options, or institutional learning.

## Economic, monetary, and allocation terms

**Economic resource** — money, property, goods, productive assets, contractual rights, or other material means.

**Economic puissance** — effective ability to mobilize economic resources for action.

**Liquidity** — ease and speed with which an asset can be converted into usable resources without excessive loss.

**Liquidity puissance** — effective ability to make resources rapidly usable.

**Allocation power** — relational power to determine which people, projects, institutions, or goals receive resources.

**Monetary power** — relational power associated with control over units, rails, issuance, settlement, reserves, access, or conditions of exchange.

**Custodial power** — relational power arising from control over assets held on behalf of others.

**Market-making puissance** — capacity to enable exchange by providing liquidity, matching, standards, custody, or transaction infrastructure.

**Property power** — relational power arising from enforceable control of scarce assets, land, infrastructure, intellectual property, or access rights.

## Information, media, network, and narrative terms

**Information resource** — data, documents, signals, archives, observations, or other transmissible representations.

**Distribution puissance** — effective ability to deliver information, resources, signals, or instructions to relevant actors at scale.

**Reach** — number and relevance of actors that can practically be reached through available channels.

**Reach puissance** — effective capacity to cross geographic, linguistic, network, or institutional boundaries.

**Attention power** — relational power to influence what receives scarce attention.

**Visibility power** — relational power to affect whether and how actors, claims, branches, or events become publicly visible.

**Agenda power** — relational power to influence what enters collective consideration as a problem, priority, or option.

**Framing power** — relational power to shape interpretation by selecting descriptions, categories, narratives, baselines, or causal frames.

**Media puissance** — effective ability to gather, package, distribute, synchronize, and amplify information to an audience.

**Network resource** — relationships, affiliations, pathways, memberships, trust links, or structural positions that can support future coordination.

**Network puissance** — effective ability to mobilize relationships for access, coordination, diffusion, recruitment, or action.

**Affiliation power** — relational effects produced by membership, association, endorsement, exclusion, or visible identification with a network or symbol.

**Narrative puissance** — effective ability to make complex systems understandable, memorable, transmissible, and emotionally or culturally legible.

**Narrative power** — relational power created when narratives shape interpretation, legitimacy, attention, identity, or mobilization.

**Brand asset** — accumulated recognition, associations, expectations, reputation, symbols, and identity attached to a name or mark.

**Brand puissance** — effective ability to mobilize brand recognition into attention, affiliation, trust, distribution, commerce, or coordination.

**Brand power** — relational effects created when brand-mediated trust, attention, certification, or affiliation changes others' opportunities, choices, or standing.

## Technical, infrastructural, administrative, and cognitive terms

**Technical resource** — code, tools, protocols, hardware, schemas, standards, credentials, deployment knowledge, or other technical assets.

**Technical puissance** — effective ability to build, modify, operate, verify, deploy, repair, or reproduce technical systems.

**Infrastructural puissance** — effective ability created by reliable shared systems, protocols, deployment paths, identity rails, data layers, or other infrastructure.

**Infrastructural power** — relational power arising when control over infrastructure materially conditions others' access, options, interoperability, or continuity.

**Administrative puissance** — effective ability to route, assign, schedule, validate, escalate, record, close, and coordinate work.

**Administrative power** — relational power produced when administrative control changes others' timing, workload, access, status, or ability to complete an action.

**Execution puissance** — effective ability to transform decisions into coordinated, traceable action.

**Cognitive puissance** — effective ability to perceive, reason, synthesize, model, plan, generate, or evaluate.

**AI-mediated cognitive puissance** — cognitive capacity amplified through AI tools under explicit governance and human/institutional constraints.

**Hidden sovereignty** — effective decision or policy control exercised by a system that is formally described as merely advisory, technical, or assistive.

## Constitutional and institutional terms

**Authority** — recognized permission or mandate to perform defined actions within a scope.

**Scoped authority** — authority limited by domain, function, duration, jurisdiction, conditions, and review.

**Mandate** — explicit basis, purpose, and boundary of legitimate authority.

**Separation of functions** — design principle preventing one role or component from silently inheriting unrelated functions.

**Functional centrality** — importance of a node to coordination or operation without necessarily implying ownership or sovereignty over the whole system.

**Sovereignty** — ultimate or non-subordinate authority within a defined domain. In this repo, technical centrality and practical dependence can create quasi-sovereign effects without formal sovereign status.

**Subsidiarity** — placing decisions and authority at the lowest level capable of handling them effectively while preserving necessary coordination.

**Federation** — coordination among independently governed nodes or institutions through shared contracts, protocols, or interoperability arrangements.

**Local sovereignty** — practical ability of a local deployment or community to govern critical operations and meanings without requiring permanent permission from a remote authority.

**Protocol commons** — shared technical or semantic substrate whose reuse and interoperability do not require submission to one commercial or political owner.

**Brand stewardship** — governance of official names, marks, certification, and identity separately from protocol ownership or operation.

## Founder and temporal terms

**Founder power** — relational leverage derived from authorship, code control, brand ownership, infrastructure, credentials, narrative authority, interpretation, relationships, or unique knowledge.

**Founder decentering** — progressive reduction of unique founder dependency through documentation, distributed mastery, independent operation, maintainer pluralism, federation, succession, and reproducibility.

**Optionality** — preserved ability to choose among future actions because resources, rights, identity, timing, or commitments have not been irreversibly fixed.

**Temporal optionality** — optionality specifically created by retaining control over timing or disclosure.

**Identity optionality** — preserved ability to reveal, separate, associate, or withhold public identity under different future conditions.

**Release optionality** — preserved ability to decide when and how a retained resource, corpus, asset, or announcement becomes active.

## kOA-specific terms

**kOA** — the broader conceptual, cultural, strategic, governance, and narrative framework.

**kOA Digital Ecosystem** — the operable digital system-of-systems supporting the cycle `Know → Choose → Act → Remember`.

**Kristal** — structured, portable, provenance-preserving knowledge artifact or family of artifacts used to make knowledge auditable, reusable, versioned, and capable of controlled transformation.

**Konnaxion** — public collaboration, circulation, coordination, and commons-facing platform layer.

**Koali** — credibility/reputation infrastructure envisioned as plural distributions around interoperable structures rather than one universal social-credit authority.

**EkoH** — domain-bounded credibility, expertise, ethics, contribution, or trust signal layer, depending on implementation context.

**SmartVote** — decision/discoverability mechanism preserving baseline and advisory/weighted readings separately rather than silently replacing political equality.

**Orgo** — operational workflow and case-management layer connecting decisions to roles, tasks, routing, escalation, closure, and operational memory.

**Capsule** — signed portable deployment/package unit intended to support local operation, verification, rollback, self-hosting, and federation.

**SemantiK Architect** — semantic-architecture function concerned with concepts, categories, relations, contexts, and multilingual structures.

**SenTient** — semantic reconciliation function concerned with ambiguity, entity linking, uncertain propositions, and structured interpretation.

## Compatibility with earlier Power Dynamics shorthand

Earlier drafts used several useful but broader expressions. They remain meaningful if interpreted through the stricter ontology.

**Power stock** — legacy shorthand for accumulated resources/assets plus durable relational positions. Prefer specifying the actual stock rather than treating power as a substance stored in one pool.

**Power flow** — broad shorthand for movement, mobilization, conversion, mediation, or transfer across the system. Prefer identifying what flows: resources, signals, attention, money, authority, puissance, effects, or returns.

**Power return** — shorthand for reusable returns produced by action or power relations. A return may become a resource, asset, relational advantage, or future puissance; it is not necessarily relational power at the moment it returns.

**Power reinvestment** — shorthand for using returns to increase future resources, capacity, puissance, or relational leverage.

**Power regeneration** — shorthand for recurring cycles in which effects are preserved and reused instead of dissipating, creating renewed or expanded puissance.

**Compounding power** — shorthand for cumulative dynamics in which returns increase later relational leverage. Prefer **compounding puissance** when the effect is increased capability without a specified relation to others.

**Power reservoir** — avoid as a strict term. Use **resource reservoir**, **epistemic reservoir**, **financial reserve**, or another domain-specific reservoir. Relational power is not stored like a physical substance.

## Repository shorthand

**Strong distributed puissance, weak sovereignty** — maximize useful effective capability while limiting non-contestable or terminal control.

**Plurality without fragmentation** — allow meaningful divergence while preserving enough shared lineage, contracts, provenance, and interoperability for comparison and recombination.

**Instance capture ≠ system capture** — failure or capture of one node should not automatically make alternatives non-viable.

**Architecture is governance before governance is written** — schemas, defaults, registries, identity systems, workflows, interfaces, and deployment topology already assign and constrain possibilities before formal policy text is applied.


## Power routing and sharing

**Power routing** — explicit design of where generated capability, authority, returns, optionality, visibility, and interpretive leverage are allowed to accumulate.

**Power redirection** — deliberate change in the default destination or conversion path of leverage, such as expertise → advice rather than sovereignty or infrastructure → local autonomy rather than operator dependency.

**Power sharing** — creation of conditions under which multiple actors can effectively use a capability or participate in a right without requiring exclusive ownership by one actor.

**Power devolution** — durable transfer of effective control from a central locus toward local, plural, or lower-level loci.

**Self-binding** — public, procedural, contractual, or technical constraints adopted by an actor or institution that make later exercises of power more legible, contestable, or limited.

**Narrative self-binding** — use of the same narrative or symbolic channel that creates attention and affiliation to publish ethical limits, anti-domination principles, attribution rules, revision norms, or other constraints on symbolic power.

**Protocolization** — conversion of tacit or founder-centric capability into explicit contracts, schemas, specifications, artifacts, tests, and reproducible behavior that others can implement independently.

**Ownership decoupling** — separation of a useful function from exclusive ownership of the implementation, brand, registry, or operator that first instantiated it.

**Capability commons** — a body of reusable capability that multiple actors can access, reproduce, improve, or instantiate without requiring one terminal owner of the function.

**Architect-confirmed design** — a current design intention confirmed in the working process but not yet supported by a canonical source document or direct implementation evidence. It must be labeled separately from source-derived or deployed claims.
