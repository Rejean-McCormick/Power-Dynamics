# Epistemic, Semantic, and Credibility Power

Epistemic power shapes what counts as sufficiently grounded knowledge for a purpose. Semantic power shapes categories, definitions and relations through which reality becomes intelligible. Credibility power shapes whose contributions are likely to be trusted, discovered or reused.

These powers are related but must not collapse into one another. A credible contributor can still be wrong. A recognized authority can make a scoped claim without owning universal truth. A semantic category can be operationally necessary while remaining contestable.

The constitutional requirement is explicit provenance, policy identity, domain-boundedness and the ability to preserve competing interpretations rather than silently replacing them.
