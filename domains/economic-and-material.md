# Economic and Material Power

Economic power is not simply possession of money. It includes liquidity, ownership, credit, capital allocation, pricing, procurement, storage, access to productive assets and the capacity to finance other forms of power.

Money is especially important because it is highly convertible: it can purchase time, labor, infrastructure, legal capacity, communication, security and experimentation. Material assets can also become symbolic or reputational capital, as the branded-gold proposals illustrate.

The key questions are who controls resources, who decides allocation, who receives returns, and whether economic contribution converts into unrelated political authority.
