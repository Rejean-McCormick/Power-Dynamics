# Recognition, Credibility, Reputation, and Certification Power

Recognition infrastructure makes evidence about expertise, contribution, conduct, reliability, qualification, or history legible. That is useful capability, but it can become standing power.

**Recognition power** is the relational ability to shape whether a capacity, contribution, or claim becomes trusted, certified, discoverable, admissible, or institutionally actionable.

## kOA mechanisms

- Koali;
- EkoH;
- evidence histories;
- domain-bounded policies;
- SmartVote advisory readings;
- certification / attestation structures.

## Main distinction

> **Score ≠ person ≠ competence ≠ ethics ≠ truth ≠ legitimacy.**
>
> **Credential claim ≠ verified credential. Popularity ≠ evidence. Citation count ≠ independent confirmation.**

A system should produce governed readings of evidence, not claim perfect measurement of human worth.

## Conversion risks

```text
credibility → visibility
credibility → opportunity
credibility → funding
credibility → political influence
```

These can be useful, but should not silently become universal authority.

Additional conversion paths include:

```text
recognition → access
recognition → opportunity
recognition → institutional permission
recognition → resource allocation
```

This means misrecognition can be materially consequential even before a market transaction occurs.

## Routing direction

Koali is intended to move toward plural distributions, portable evidence, explicit policy identity, interoperability, and independent operation rather than terminal founder ownership.


## Authority-signal corruption

Credibility systems are vulnerable when surface signals can be converted into standing without tracing their basis. Titles, publication formats, follower counts, endorsements, and citation volume can all create useful heuristics, but they can also be manufactured, inherited, or recursively amplified.

The constitutional objective is not to eliminate heuristics. It is to prevent **prestige signals from silently becoming truth claims** and to preserve enough provenance for independent evaluation.


## Recognition wedge

Do not define the recognition problem as "real human value minus score." Instead, choose a bounded access or opportunity variable `X` and compare observed recognition with an explicit evidence-calibrated counterfactual:

```text
W_recognition = X_calibrated-evidence - X_observed
```

Both under-recognition and over-recognition matter. Prestige, inherited status, institutional brand, popularity, or accumulated visibility can be over-weighted just as unknown contributors can be under-recognized.

## Contextual ethics, not a universal moral score

Ethical or safety-relevant evidence can matter in some roles, but a single scalar "ethical score" should not become a general permission layer. Different contexts require different evidence and different legitimate decision authorities.

For high-impact access, prefer domain-bounded evidence, explicit criteria, provenance, contestation, correction, minimal disclosure, time sensitivity, and separation between evidence systems and final authority. Fundamental standing should not depend on one general reputation score.

## Recognition as a toll bridge

A useful recognition system can become extractive if leaving it makes employment, finance, participation, or social standing impracticable. The core warning pattern is:

```text
useful recognition
→ dominant recognition
→ required recognition
→ constrained exit
→ gatekeeping power
```

The constitutional response is plural policy, portable evidence, interoperability, local operation where feasible, and credible alternatives.
