# Administrative, Operational, and Temporal Power

Administrative power determines how decisions become cases, responsibilities, deadlines, approvals, escalations and closure. It often appears neutral while controlling the practical fate of formal decisions.

Temporal power matters because delay, deadlines, waiting costs and ownership of human time can expand or shrink effective possibility. A right delivered after the relevant opportunity has disappeared may be formally intact and practically useless.

Orgo is therefore politically significant: routing, escalation and closure rules are not merely workflow details; they are allocations of operational power.
