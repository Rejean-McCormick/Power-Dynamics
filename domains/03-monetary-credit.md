# Monetary, Credit, and Liquidity Power

Monetary power is not identical to economic power. It concerns control over the infrastructure and conditions of exchange.

## Monetary surfaces

- currency issuance;
- units of account;
- liquidity;
- settlement;
- payment rails;
- reserves;
- convertibility;
- credit access;
- debt terms;
- transaction permissions.

A monetary operator can alter effective possibility without owning all underlying economic assets because other actors depend on the rail itself.

## Why it is high leverage

```text
rail access
→ ability to transact
→ business continuity
→ economic dependency
→ bargaining / political leverage
```

## Legacy stress test

KingCoin legacy proposals combine monetary governance, taxation, redistribution, sanctions, public investment, and dispute enforcement. In current doctrine this is not a recommendation; it is a useful demonstration of **power stacking and terminality risk**.

## Current constitutional lesson

> Powerful monetary functions require especially strong separation, substitution, transparency, and limits on cross-domain conversion.
