# Effective Possibility

The framework's central object is not abstract freedom but **effective possibility**.

A formal option can exist while remaining practically unusable. A person may possess a legal right but lack time, money, information, institutional recognition, language access, transportation, technical capability, or a viable appeal process. Conversely, infrastructure can expand effective possibility by reducing those barriers.

This is why dependency is central. If essential possibilities depend on an actor whose rules are opaque, unilateral, difficult to contest, or costly to exit, that dependency creates relational leverage even if the actor rarely intervenes.

Effective possibility should be assessed through access, affordability, time cost, intelligibility, technical operability, legal recognition, portability, alternatives, coordination cost, reversibility, risk, and institutional acceptance.

The objective is not perfect quantification. It is to make the relevant constraints and enabling conditions **legible**.
