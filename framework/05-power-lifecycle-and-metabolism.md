# Power Lifecycle and Metabolism

Power is not only held or exercised. It has a lifecycle.

```text
Resource
→ Latent Capacity
→ Effective Power
→ Exercise
→ Outcome
→ Return
→ Asset
→ Reinvestment
→ New Capacity
```

A resource becomes power only when it can be mobilized. Expertise nobody can discover is latent. Money locked behind unusable procedure may be technically owned but not operationally available. Code without deployability may have low effective technical power.

Exercise then produces more than an immediate outcome. It can leave durable assets behind: experience, evidence, code, documentation, relationships, trust, data, brand recognition, procedures or cash. These assets become **power returns** when they improve later capability.

Reinvestment is the process by which returns are deliberately fed back into another part of the ecology. Revenue may fund translation. Experience may improve architecture. A successful pilot may produce institutional access. Documentation may reduce onboarding cost. Brand recognition may attract contributors.

The concept of **power metabolism** captures the whole cycle: the system consumes resources, converts them into action, produces outputs and by-products, and either loses or recycles those by-products into future capability.

The analytical question is not simply whether a system produces value, but whether its metabolism is regenerative, extractive, wasteful, concentrated or broadly compounding.
