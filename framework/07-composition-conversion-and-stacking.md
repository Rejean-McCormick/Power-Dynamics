# Composition, Conversion, and Stacking

Three dynamics must be separated.

## Composition

Multiple capabilities combine to create a higher-order capability without one necessarily replacing another. This is the core logic of `power with`.

## Conversion

One form of capital or power becomes another. Examples:

- money → infrastructure;
- expertise → credibility;
- credibility → discoverability;
- discoverability → opportunity;
- brand → sales;
- adoption → standards power;
- technical maintenance → default-setting power.

Conversions are not inherently bad. The problem is **silent or unbounded conversion**.

## Stacking

Several distinct legitimate powers accumulate in the same actor or node. A bank that becomes simultaneously financial intermediary, identity authority, credential authority, philanthropic rail and platform gateway illustrates power stacking. Each role may be defensible separately while their combination creates dependency and cross-domain leverage.

Power Dynamics therefore asks not only whether each function is legitimate, but whether their composition creates a new level of power that no individual mandate anticipated.
