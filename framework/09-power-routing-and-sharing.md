# Power Routing and Sharing

Power Dynamics studies not only the **generation** and **constraint** of power, but its **routing**.

> **Power routing is the explicit design of where generated capability, authority, returns, and optionality are allowed to accumulate.**

This adds a missing question to ordinary power analysis. It is not enough to identify a source of power and ask how it is limited. A system can often redirect the same source into a different institutional destination.

## Core terms

### Power redirection

A deliberate change in the default destination or conversion path of leverage.

Examples:

- expertise → advice rather than sovereignty;
- capital → shared funding rather than extra votes;
- infrastructure → user autonomy rather than operator dependency;
- founder knowledge → protocol rather than permanent personal gatekeeping.

### Power sharing

The creation of conditions under which multiple actors can effectively use a capability or participate in a decision-right without requiring exclusive ownership by one actor.

Sharing can concern capability, authority, returns, or optionality.

### Power devolution

A durable transfer of effective control from a central locus toward local, plural, or lower-level loci.

### Self-binding

A mechanism by which a powerful actor or institution publishes, encodes, or accepts constraints that make later exercises of power more legible, contestable, or limited.

### Protocolization

The conversion of tacit, founder-centric, or institution-centric capability into explicit contracts, schemas, specifications, portable artifacts, and reproducible behavior that others can implement or operate.

### Ownership decoupling

The separation of a useful function from exclusive ownership of the implementation, brand, registry, or operator that first instantiated it.

## Routing dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Capability | Who can actually do more because the system exists? |
| Authority | Who can approve, validate, rank, route, certify, update, or decide? |
| Returns | Who receives the assets generated by successful action? |
| Optionality | Who can refuse, leave, substitute, fork, self-host, or wait? |
| Interpretation | Who can define categories, credibility policies, readings, and meanings? |
| Visibility | Who controls discoverability, ranking, attention, and agenda? |

A system is not meaningfully power-sharing merely because one dimension is open. Open code can coexist with closed identity, centralized ranking, proprietary credibility, or terminal network effects.

## Redirection matrix

| Concentration vector | Redirection pattern |
|---|---|
| founder mastery | protocolization + documentation + maintainer pluralism |
| brand / narrative | public ethical self-binding + architecture-first references |
| credibility | plural distributions + portable evidence + contestable policy |
| expertise | bounded advisory influence + preserved political baseline |
| knowledge validation | provenance + reader plurality + competing artifacts |
| infrastructure | portability + self-hosting + federation + substitution |
| capital | contribution separated from political standing |
| incumbent branch | branch mobility + compatibility + demonstrable replacement |
| AI cognition | explicit policy + provenance + human-governed authority boundaries |

## Self-binding is not self-certification

Publishing principles is not proof that they are followed.

A self-binding mechanism becomes stronger when commitments are:

- explicit;
- stable enough to cite;
- connected to procedures;
- auditable against behavior;
- revisable through a visible process;
- usable by critics and participants as grounds for contestation.

This distinction is essential for narrative ethics. Ethical principles inside fiction can constrain symbolic drift by making the narrative carry its own limits, but they do not replace institutional safeguards.

## Sharing without flattening

Power sharing does not require identical influence or identical expertise.

The target is **non-terminal asymmetry**: differences may exist, but affected actors retain meaningful routes to understand, contest, substitute, leave, learn, reproduce, or build alternatives.

## Relation to the constitution

Traditional constitutional design asks how to constrain authority after it forms.

Power routing asks an earlier question:

> **Can the architecture prevent some authority from concentrating in the first place by routing capability somewhere else?**

The full constitutional sequence becomes:

> **Generate → Route → Scope → Bind → Verify → Contest → Substitute → Exit → Fork → Evolve**
