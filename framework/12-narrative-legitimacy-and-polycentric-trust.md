# Narrative legitimacy and polycentric trust

This chapter connects three otherwise separable questions:

1. How can materially unequal orders preserve legitimacy?
2. How can people distinguish interpretation, selective truth, and evidentiary claims?
3. How can autonomous institutions recognize information and one another without creating a terminal truth authority?

It is an analytical and design proposal, **not** evidence of deployed kOA behavior.

## 1. Power maintenance has a cognitive dimension

Economic, coercive, judicial, administrative, and informational leverage can reinforce one another. Institutions can outlast severe hardship if they retain financial capacity, organized support, and enough perceived legitimacy. Conversely, a crisis of legitimacy can undermine a materially functioning order. There is no deterministic inequality-to-revolution threshold.

A useful heuristic is:

```text
concentration of resources / access
  -> possible influence over agenda, institutions, and information visibility
  -> recurring interpretations of what is normal, deserved, feasible
  -> public legitimacy or contested legitimacy
  -> cooperation, resignation, reform, resistance, or exit
  -> feedback into institutional arrangements
```

This is a set of possible mechanisms, not a universal causal loop. Armed force, electoral rules, organizational capacity, divisions among elites, material conditions, and international pressures remain independent variables.

## 2. Weaponization without literal falsehood

A truthful success story can be unrepresentative. Selecting the exceptional upwardly mobile individual while omitting base rates tells an emotionally compelling story that cannot by itself establish equal opportunity. Similar failures occur with:

- **survivorship selection:** displaying visible winners rather than the population of attempts;
- **agenda selection:** sustained visibility for one harm and obscurity for another;
- **framing:** choosing the denominator, causal explanation, or moral category;
- **omission:** hiding a relevant distribution, period, comparator, uncertainty, or counterevidence;
- **repetition and evidence laundering:** many echoes that originate in a single unsupported report;
- **prestige transfer:** treating an issuer's standing as proof for claims beyond its remit;
- **emotional mobilization:** reassurance, pride, fear, or resentment used to induce agreement.

These patterns do not imply malign intent in each instance. They may arise from market incentives, editorial choices, social identities, or genuine disagreement. An audit must demonstrate a specific distortion rather than assume a conspiracy.

**Epistemic rule:** possibility != prevalence; popularity != truth; authenticity != accuracy; institutional recognition != blanket endorsement; disagreement != disinformation.

## 3. From narrative dependence to understanding and agency

The proposed UCKK -> kOA relationship is:

```text
UCKK: question -> inspect -> compare -> understand -> revise
  +
kOA: discover -> organize -> deliberate -> decide -> act -> remember
  =
collective capability without a mandatory central worldview
```

`Gather to Liberate` describes the social aspiration: plural actors can cooperate across disagreements, establish shared procedures, and leave or form alternatives. Narrative culture (including King Klown) may lower the entry barrier, but fictional or symbolic authority must not become mandatory epistemic or institutional authority.

## 4. Institutions as scoped, reciprocal recognition centers

Let R(A,B,D,T,P) express: *reader institution A chooses, under policy P, to accept a specified class T of attestations from issuer B within domain D*.

- **Directed:** R(A,B,...) does not imply R(B,A,...).
- **Scoped:** a publisher may be authoritative on its product documentation without being an independent authority on its product's economic value.
- **Selective:** A can accept B's engineering credentials but decline B's political claims.
- **Versioned and revocable:** policies and credentials change; their prior decisions should remain auditable.
- **Non-transitive by default:** A trusting B and B trusting C does not authorize automatic A-to-C trust unless A expressly delegates such authority within bounds.
- **Plural:** readers and institutions can maintain incompatible *policies* while retaining compatible *exchange protocols*.

A declaration from UNESCO, Harvard, a city, a sports club, a lab, or Microsoft is an example of an issuer class. It is not automatically a valid claim, and the named institutions have **not** been asserted to participate in kOA.

## 5. Four independent layers of evaluation

| Layer | Question | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance | Who authored/issued this and is the artifact intact? | signatures, hashes, official publication, archival copy |
| Claim support | What does the evidence actually establish? | primary records, method, denominators, counterevidence |
| Reader policy | Does this reader accept the issuer and type of evidence? | scoped issuer rules, disclosed delegations, policy version |
| Legitimacy / applicability | Is acceptance warranted in this jurisdiction/use? | law, role, domain competence, rights and appeal processes |

None of these layers substitutes for the others. An institution can be widely recognized and wrong; an unpopular minority can be right; a valid mathematical proof does not confer legal authority.

## 6. Institutional evolution without mandatory overthrow

A proposed escape from terminal institutional power is **parallel institution-building**:

```text
existing institution A retains its followers
                | \
  confidence weakens   some participants remain
                |
  independently deployable software + portable evidence
                |
 new institution B establishes policies and competence
                |
 external institutions freely accept some of B's attestations
                |
 plural legitimacy, competition, cooperation, or replacement
```

Replication of code, schemas, and openly licensed artifacts **does not** clone an organization's legal status, historical evidence, reputation, trademark, statutory authority, or proprietary records. Effective exit must be tested under adverse conditions, including founder capture, platform disappearance, network effects, and standards capture.

Power centers therefore remain possible and often useful. The constitutional test is whether their influence is **bounded, contestable, revisable, and non-terminal**.

## 7. Media-disagreement analysis

A comparison system should decompose articles into individually reviewable claims and record:

- original statement; exact quotation or faithful paraphrase;
- issuer identity, publication time, edits, source lineage;
- category: empirical, predictive, causal, opinion, value judgment, or undefined;
- base rate/denominator, period, population and uncertainty;
- independent and dependent evidentiary lineages;
- sources cited and salient counterevidence omitted;
- fact-checker's criteria, domain of expertise, policy version;
- outcome: supported, contradicted, partly supported, out-of-scope, or unresolved.

**A media comparison is a map of claims and their support, not a plebiscite of truth.** A 100-to-1 consensus of copied claims has less evidentiary independence than two independent primary datasets.

## 8. Falsification / failure conditions

This framework must confront outcomes in which: users are less able to distinguish raw facts from trust-policy outputs; forked institutions cannot exchange or retain data; popularity dominates evidentiary support; delegation silently expands across domains; revocation erases historical records; high-status issuers receive immunity from criticism; or malicious entities generate mutually reinforcing attestations.
