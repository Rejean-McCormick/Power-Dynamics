# Thesis

Power Dynamics treats power as **effective possibility**: the practical capacity to make something possible, probable, visible, credible, coordinated, enforceable, difficult, costly, or impossible.

This definition is deliberately broader than authority. A person may hold formal rights while lacking the time, money, information or procedural access needed to exercise them. Conversely, a platform may hold no constitutional office while controlling visibility, discoverability or dependency in ways that materially shape collective action.

The framework therefore asks two symmetrical questions:

- **What becomes possible because this actor, relation or infrastructure exists?**
- **Whose possibilities become narrower, conditional or dependent because it exists?**

Power is neither presumed good nor presumed bad. `Power to`, `power with`, and `power through` can generate new agency. `Power over` may be legitimate when scoped and accountable, but dangerous when arbitrary, opaque, terminal or convertible into unrelated authority.

kOA is analyzed as a power architecture because its stated purpose is to structure the transitions through which a group knows, chooses, acts and remembers. When those transitions become stable and reusable, distributed resources become collective capability. That capability is itself a new pole of power.

The normative problem is therefore not to eliminate power. It is to make power legible enough that its formation, scope, conversion, returns, concentration, alternatives and failure modes can be governed.
