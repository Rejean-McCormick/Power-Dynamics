# Capitals, Assets, and Returns

Power Dynamics distinguishes **capital** from **power**.

Capital is a stored or reusable resource that can produce capability. Power is the effective capacity actually available in a situation.

Relevant capitals include:

- financial capital;
- time;
- knowledge and epistemic capital;
- technical assets and code;
- operational routines;
- credibility and reputation;
- social and network capital;
- institutional access;
- brand and symbolic capital;
- memory;
- data;
- legal rights;
- infrastructure;
- optionality.

The distinction avoids category errors. Expertise is not itself political authority. Brand recognition is not legitimacy. Money is not influence until a conversion path makes it so.

## Returns

Every significant exercise should be analyzed for what it leaves behind.

A project may yield a product **and** experience. A public campaign may yield attention **and** a network. A donation rail may yield funded programs **and** institutional trust. A software build may yield code, schemas, deployment patterns and reduced future development cost.

The critical political question is:

> **Who receives the returns generated by collective activity?**

If participants create the value while an operator captures almost all reusable returns, the system may become progressively extractive. If returns remain portable and reusable by contributors and communities, the same activity can increase distributed capacity.
