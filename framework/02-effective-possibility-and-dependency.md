# Effective Possibility and Dependency

The framework distinguishes **formal possibility** from **effective possibility**.

A formal right can be practically hollow when exercising it requires resources the person does not possess. A complaint process can exist while procedural complexity, legal cost and time pressure make it unusable. A market can offer nominal choice while debt, employment dependence or housing scarcity make refusal too costly.

Dependency becomes politically important when one actor controls a resource, procedure or infrastructure that another must use to preserve important capabilities. The relationship is especially concerning when the dependent party cannot inspect the rules, contest outcomes, substitute the provider, or exit without disproportionate loss.

This yields a general relation:

> **Dependency = reliance on another actor or system for the preservation of effective possibility.**

Dependency is not automatically domination. People and institutions are interdependent by nature. The question is whether the dependency is **governable**.

Useful tests include:

- Can the dependent party understand the conditions?
- Can it contest a decision?
- Can it substitute another provider or branch?
- Can it leave without losing essential assets?
- Can the dependency be reduced by portability or local operation?
- Can the powerful actor alter the relationship unilaterally?

This is why offline capability, open formats, provenance and forkability are political properties rather than merely technical conveniences.
