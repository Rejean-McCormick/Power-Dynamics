# Power Modes

Power Dynamics uses four basic modes as analytical lenses rather than mutually exclusive categories.

## Power over

Capacity to constrain, condition, rank, exclude, block, compel, delay or make choices costly for another actor. It includes formal authority but also control of infrastructure, attention, money, procedure and access.

## Power to

Capacity to accomplish something: understand a situation, create, refuse, organize, obtain resources, solve a problem or act on a decision.

## Power with

Capacity that arises from coordinated plurality. A lawyer, mechanic, translator, local resident and programmer may together possess a capability none has individually. Collective intelligence becomes political power when such composition can be made durable and executable.

## Power through

Capacity mediated or amplified by infrastructure. Search, banking, communication networks, Konnaxion, Kristal, Orgo or an AI assistant can let an actor do something it could not efficiently do alone.

The modes often coexist. A system can increase users' `power to` while simultaneously increasing operator `power over`. Power analysis should therefore resist simple labels such as "empowering" or "centralized" and instead map the different relations separately.
