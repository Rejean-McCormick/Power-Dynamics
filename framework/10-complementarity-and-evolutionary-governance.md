# Complementarity and Evolutionary Governance

The system is not expected to reach a final perfect branch. It should instead preserve the capability to improve.

A branch may diverge because of new evidence, a different context, alternative values, better implementation, different risk tolerance or a legitimate disagreement about interpretation. Divergence is therefore not automatically corruption or failure.

The evolutionary cycle is:

```text
imperfection or opportunity
→ criticism
→ variation
→ demonstration
→ comparison
→ support / adoption
→ coexistence, merge, influence or replacement
→ further variation
```

A branch claiming improvement should specify **what it improves**, for whom, under which conditions, and at what cost. Popularity alone is not proof. A branch may be superior on autonomy while another is superior on ease of use. Several may lie on a complementary frontier with no universal winner.

This produces **evolutionary governance**: governance in which rules, policies, interpretations and implementations remain open to explicit variation, comparison, recombination and replacement.

The stable core should therefore protect the *capacity to evolve* — lineage, provenance, interoperability, version identity, portability and contestability — rather than freeze every substantive outcome.

> **The core protects evolution; it does not freeze truth.**
