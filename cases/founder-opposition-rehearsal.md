# Founder Opposition Rehearsal

A system is not founder-independent merely because its code can run without the founder. It must also remain capable of **disagreeing with, organizing against, and defeating the founder's proposals** when those proposals are wrong.

Current architect-confirmed design includes a deliberately adversarial civic drill built around a politically coherent party that contains one conspicuously abusive policy: a state prohibition on tattoos combined with compulsory removal of existing tattoos.

The policy is not the point of the exercise. The intended test is whether founder or persona authority can be converted into political leverage **without becoming political immunity**.

## Power conversion under test

```text
founder authorship / symbolic capital / rhetorical skill
→ attention
→ trust / deference
→ agenda influence
→ political mobilization
→ attempted policy leverage
```

The constitutional requirement is that this chain remain interruptible.

```text
attention ≠ truth
trust ≠ immunity from criticism
founder status ≠ policy authority
movement coherence ≠ mandatory agreement
```

## Civic fire-drill logic

A fire drill creates practiced evacuation pathways before a real fire exists. This case applies the same logic to counterpower.

The intended pathway is:

```text
problematic proposal
→ recognition
→ objection
→ evidence and rights analysis
→ argument registry
→ relevant expertise
→ coalition formation
→ institutional contestation
→ amendment / rejection
→ postmortem
→ reusable civic memory
```

The desired outcome is not hostility to the founder. It is **legitimate divisible agreement**: participants can support a broader project while rejecting and organizing against a specific proposal.

## Rehearsed counterpower

Power Dynamics distinguishes **formal counterpower** from **rehearsed counterpower**.

A right to contest that has never been exercised may remain socially expensive, procedurally unclear, or symbolically taboo. A successful drill can establish a precedent that opposition to the founder is normal, legitimate, and survivable.

The resulting pattern can become a reusable reference — informally, a **Tattoo Protocol** — for later cases in which an authority makes a subtler or genuinely unintended error.

## Success criteria

The drill is successful if:

- the proposal can be isolated from the rest of the platform;
- critics can organize without being treated as enemies of the whole project;
- evidence, rights, proportionality, expertise, and consequences can be made legible;
- founder symbolic power does not silently become sovereign authority;
- opposition can succeed without requiring censorship or destruction of the surrounding movement;
- the process leaves reusable institutional memory.

## Failure modes

Important failure modes include:

- supporters rationalize the proposal primarily because of founder loyalty;
- critics must exit the whole system to oppose one proposal;
- persona prestige overrides evidence or rights;
- counterpower exists formally but is too costly to exercise;
- the exercise creates a precedent of obedience rather than a precedent of contestation.

The constitutional objective is:

> **A system should not merely permit opposition to its founder. It should know how to practice it.**
