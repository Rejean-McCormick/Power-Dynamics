# Improvement, Support, Adoption, and Dominance

An alternative should be able to say not only “we disagree,” but **what changed and what value was added**.

Possible demonstrations include stronger evidence, better provenance, lower error, lower cost, higher autonomy, better accessibility, improved multilinguality, better outcomes, stronger privacy, better local fit, or improved interoperability.

Support, adoption, and dominance are distinct. A branch may be intellectually supported but rarely deployed, or dominant because of history, compatibility, network effects, official status, or funding.

> **Dominance ≠ Truth ≠ Quality ≠ Legitimacy**

No universal scalar is required. Improvement can be multidimensional and context-dependent.
