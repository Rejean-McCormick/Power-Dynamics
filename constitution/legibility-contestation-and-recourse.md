# Legibility, Contestation, and Recourse

Power that cannot be reconstructed is difficult to govern.

Legibility requires enough information to answer:

- what rule was applied;
- which evidence entered;
- which version of the policy was active;
- which actor or authority performed the action;
- what changed;
- who was affected;
- how the outcome can be challenged.

Contestation is more than the ability to complain. Effective recourse needs an identified forum, reasonable cost, a reversible or correctable outcome, and a record of the challenge.

For reputation and expertise systems, a user should be able to contest evidence, domain assignment, policy interpretation and the resulting signal separately. For knowledge systems, a challenge should not erase the original claim; it should preserve lineage and disagreement.
