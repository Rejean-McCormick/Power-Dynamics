# Scoped Authority and Separation of Functions

Authority is legitimate only within a declared domain, mandate and procedure.

A validator may validate under a policy without becoming sovereign over unrelated knowledge. An expert may provide a stronger advisory signal in a domain without receiving extra political citizenship. An Orgo role may execute a mandate without redefining the mandate.

Separation of functions is therefore a power architecture. It should be possible to identify separately:

- who defines the mandate;
- who supplies evidence;
- who validates;
- who recommends;
- who decides;
- who executes;
- who audits;
- who can revise or revoke each role.

The more of these functions collapse into one actor, the greater the need to analyze stacking, dependency and conversion risk.
