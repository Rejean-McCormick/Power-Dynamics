# Authority Architecture: Scope, Separation, and Centrality

Legitimate authority should specify domain, function, jurisdiction, duration, basis, conditions, review, delegation, and revocation.

Authority in one domain should not automatically travel to another. A technical maintainer may authorize releases without deciding political outcomes. An expert may provide domain-bounded advice without receiving universal civic superiority.

Separation of functions limits conversion by assigning distinct responsibilities to distinct components or roles. Examples include separating credibility from vote results, narrative from legitimacy, execution from mandate creation, protocol commons from brand stewardship, and AI assistance from final authority.

Healthy systems also need not be centerless. A useful pattern is:

> **StrongHub + DistributedCapability + ReplicableWhole**

Important distinctions:

- Functional centrality ≠ domination
- Centralized access ≠ centralized truth
- Strong center ≠ total sovereignty
- Federation ≠ flatness
- Local sovereignty ≠ absence of a local center
- Composition does not require ownership
- Creator-owned channel ≠ mandatory dependency
- Independent reproducibility ≠ founder removal

The constitutional question is whether actors can verify, substitute, reproduce, branch, and continue when the center changes or fails.
