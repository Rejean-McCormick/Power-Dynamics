# Power Redirection and Sharing

The Power Constitution is not only defensive.

It does not wait for a powerful center to emerge and then attempt to constrain it. It also asks how architecture can **redirect leverage before it becomes terminal**.

## Constitutional objective

> **Generate strong distributed puissance, route its returns and control deliberately, and prevent useful coordination from becoming exclusive sovereignty.**

## Six constitutional mechanisms

### 1. Conversion firewalls

Prevent one legitimate form of capability from silently becoming unrelated authority.

### 2. Power routing

Specify where capability, authority, returns, and optionality are intended to accumulate.

### 3. Self-binding

Make principles, procedures, limits, and revision rules public enough that later behavior can be compared against them.

### 4. Protocolization

Translate unique personal or institutional mastery into reusable, testable, and independently implementable forms.

### 5. Devolution and plurality

Permit local operation, multiple distributions, branches, reader policies, or implementations rather than one mandatory operator.

### 6. Counterpower

Preserve verification, contestation, substitution, exit, forkability, and branch mobility when routing fails or capture occurs.

## Public ethics as a power mechanism

Publishing ethical principles in a symbolic or fictional layer is politically relevant when the symbolic layer itself creates influence.

The mechanism is:

```text
narrative puissance
→ explicit public commitments
→ interpretable limits
→ expectations and criticism
→ self-binding of symbolic authority
```

This is stronger than private intention because the commitment becomes externalized and reusable by others.

It remains weaker than enforceable institutional governance unless tied to procedures, audit, recourse, or material control.

## Protocol commons and founder power

A founder may legitimately create, coordinate, steward, or brand a system while still redirecting much of the system's practical capability outward.

The desired transition is:

> **Founder-powered → Architecture-powered → Protocol-powered → Ecosystem-powered**

Independent reproducibility is the key test.

## Koali as a power-sharing test

Credibility infrastructure is especially sensitive because it can affect opportunity, standing, discoverability, and access.

A non-terminal design should make it possible for multiple Koali distributions or compatible implementations to exist without one founder or operator owning the only meaningful credibility layer.

The audit questions are:

- Can another group instantiate the function?
- Can evidence and history move?
- Can policy differ while identity and provenance remain interpretable?
- Can alternative distributions become discoverable and viable?
- Can one operator deny interoperability to all competitors?
- Does the official brand control the protocol, or only a particular distribution?

## Failure modes

Power-sharing language can hide concentration if:

- the code is open but the registry is not;
- forks exist but network access does not;
- principles are public but behavior is not auditable;
- data is portable but identity or reputation is not;
- local hosting exists but updates require one signing authority;
- multiple credibility models exist but one platform controls discoverability;
- democratic allocation exists but capital controls eligibility upstream.

For that reason, power redirection must be assessed as an **effective system property**, not a slogan.
