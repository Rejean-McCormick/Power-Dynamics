# Stable Kernel, Evolving Branches

Not every part of a system should evolve at the same speed.

The **stable kernel** should preserve the conditions that allow meaningful divergence:

- artifact identity;
- provenance;
- version lineage;
- basic interchange contracts;
- cryptographic integrity where required;
- explicit status and policy identity;
- mechanisms for comparison and migration.

**Evolving branches** may differ in:

- reader policies;
- credibility models;
- domain taxonomies;
- ranking rules;
- validation thresholds;
- governance procedures;
- interfaces;
- institutional workflows.

The kernel becomes dangerous when it silently expands from interoperability into substantive sovereignty. Branches become dangerous when they break all common structure and make comparison impossible. The constitutional target is **plurality without fragmentation**.
